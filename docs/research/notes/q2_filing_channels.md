- **[Confirmed]**: backed by a BIR issuance, or the issuance's text as reproduced by a BIR digest or law-firm advisory. Issuance and URL are given.
- **[Interpretation]**: my reading of confirmed sources.
- **[Needs verification]**: secondary or blog source only, sources conflict, or not found.

## 1. Who must use eFPS, who uses eBIRForms, and must self-employed professionals e-file?

### Takeaway

eFPS is mandatory only for listed categories: large taxpayers, the Top 20,000 corporations, the Top 5,000 individuals, TAMP taxpayers, NGAs, government bidders, and others. An ordinary self-employed professional or sole proprietor is normally a non-eFPS taxpayer. They must still file electronically, through the Offline eBIRForms package or a BIR-certified Tax Software Provider (TSP), because since EOPT/RR 4-2024 the rule is that all returns are e-filed. Manual filing is now an exception.

### Cited Findings

- **[Confirmed – secondary reproduction of the BIR list]** The eFPS-mandated categories and their issuances are:
    - TAMP taxpayers (RR 10-2014)
    - Accredited importers and prospective importers needing a BIR-ICC/BCC (RR 10-2014)
    - National Government Agencies (RR 1-2013)
    - All licensed local contractors (RR 10-2012)
    - Enterprises with fiscal incentives such as PEZA and BOI (RR 1-2010)
    - Top 5,000 individual taxpayers (RR 6-2009)
    - Corporations with paid-up capital of P10M or more (RR 10-2007)
    - Corporations with a complete CAS (RR 10-2007)
    - Procuring government agencies, for withholding of VAT and percentage tax (RR 3-2005)
    - Government bidders (RR 3-2005)
    - Insurance companies and stock brokers (RMC 71-2004)
    - Large taxpayers (RR 2-2002, as amended)
    - Top 20,000 private corporations

    Source: [Roque Law – Reminder for taxpayers required to use eFPS](https://roquelaw.com.ph/reminder-for-taxpayers-required-to-use-efps/). The list is also said to be restated in RMC 4-2021: [search summary citing RMC 4-2021](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/who-is-required-to-use-the-bir-efps-in-the-philippines) (secondary).

- **[Confirmed – secondary]** RR 10-2014 amended Section 3 of RR 9-2001 to add TAMP taxpayers and accredited importers. It was effective for returns filed from 1 Jan 2015 or 15 days after publication, whichever was later. — [Grant Thornton – Mandatory eFPS coverage for accredited importers and TAMP taxpayers](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/alerts-and-publications/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2014/mandatory-efps-coverage-for-accredited-importers-and-tamp-taxpayers/); [RR 10-2014 PDF (GT)](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/globalassets/1.-member-firms/philippines/tax-alerts/2014/12.11.2014-2-3/rr-10-2014.pdf)
- **[Confirmed – secondary]** RR 6-2014, as amended by RR 5-2015, originally mandated eBIRForms for specific groups:
    - accredited tax agents/practitioners and all their client-taxpayers
    - accredited printers of receipts/invoices
    - one-time-transaction (ONETT) taxpayers
    - "no payment" return filers
    - GOCCs, LGUs and cooperatives

    Sources: [Grant Thornton – Penalties for failure to file tax returns under eFPS and eBIRForms (2015)](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/alerts-and-publications/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2015/penalties-for-failure-to-file-tax-returns-under-efps-and-ebirforms/); [Inquirer – BIR to strictly implement e-filing system](https://business.inquirer.net/188934/bir-to-strictly-implement-e-filing-system). A Deloitte summary of RMC 19-2015 notes that, at that time, only the listed taxpayers were mandated to enroll in eBIRForms and others could still file manually: [Deloitte – RMC 19-2015](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/Deloitte/ph/Documents/tax/ph-tax-rmc-no-19-2015-noexp.pdf). **This is prior law. EOPT has since superseded it in practice; see Section 3.**

- **[Confirmed]** RMC 20-2026 (16 Mar 2026), the CY2025 AITR guidelines, sets the filing modes:
    - **eFPS**: for "taxpayers mandated to use the system or voluntarily opted to enroll". They pay through the eFPS-AAB where they are enrolled.
    - **Offline eBIRForms Package v7.9.5**: for "non-eFPS taxpayers, including those filing 'No Payment' returns".
    - **BIR-certified TSPs**: for specific returns.

    Source: [BIR Digest of RMC 20-2026 (bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph)](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RMC%20No.%2020-2026%20Digest.pdf)

- **[Confirmed]** The certified TSPs listed in RMC 20-2026 (each is certified only for certain forms, per Annex A) are:
    - Aktus (ETAX PH)
    - Ananta (ANANTAKS)
    - Asian Consulting Group (TAXWHIZPH)
    - Carl Patrick E. Cubillas (TAXISTA)
    - eTaxPoint
    - MPM Consulting
    - MyTaxWhizPH
    - Taxumo
    - Ten Eleven Manila (JuanTax)
    - Wyvern (WYVERN-ONETT)
    - Yahshua (Yahshua Tax Online)

    Source: [BDB Law advisory reproducing RMC 20-2026](https://www.bdblaw.com.ph/images/advisory/190_Advisory_RMC_No20-2026.pdf)

- **[Confirmed]** An eFPS-mandated taxpayer must use eBIRForms instead of eFPS when:
    - eFPS/eFPS-AAB enrollment is still in process;
    - the enhanced form is not yet in eFPS;
    - eFPS is down, under a BIR advisory; or
    - the eFPS-AAB is down, under an AAB advisory.

    Source: [BIR Digest of RMC 20-2026](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RMC%20No.%2020-2026%20Digest.pdf)

- **[Confirmed]** Medium or large individual taxpayers "shall file electronically the AITR using BIR Form No. 1701 or 1701A". — [BDB Law advisory reproducing RMC 20-2026, Sec. IV](https://www.bdblaw.com.ph/images/advisory/190_Advisory_RMC_No20-2026.pdf)

### Inferences

- **[Interpretation]** A typical self-employed professional or sole proprietor is not eFPS-mandated. The exceptions are being in the Top 5,000 individuals, being tagged TAMP, being a government bidder or licensed contractor, or having fiscal incentives. Such a taxpayer e-files through Offline eBIRForms or a certified TSP. The app should ask "Are you enrolled in or notified for eFPS?" rather than assume.
- **[Interpretation]** "Are self-employed professionals required to e-file?" Yes, by default. Under RR 4-2024 (Section 3), returns are to be filed electronically. The paper exceptions are narrow: system downtime, a form not yet available electronically, Commissioner authorization, and the 1701-MS micro/small option (Section 2).

- I found no 2025–2026 issuance that adds new eFPS-mandated categories. **[Needs verification]**

## 2. Is manual (paper) filing at AABs/RDOs still allowed, for whom, and what penalties apply?

### Takeaway

Paper filing is allowed only in three cases: under a BIR advisory that the e-systems are down, with a justifiable reason accepted by the CIR, or when the return is not yet on any e-platform. Separately, RMC 20-2026 expressly lets micro and small individual taxpayers file BIR Form 1701-MS manually. With tax payable, it goes to any AAB; without tax payable, to any RDO. The old P1,000-per-return penalty and the 25% wrong-venue surcharge for mandated e-filers who filed manually date from 2015. The 25% wrong-venue part has since been removed by EOPT.

### Cited Findings

- **[Confirmed]** RMC 20-2026 says "Manual filing shall only be allowed under the following instances":
    - "When there is a BIR-issued Advisory on the unavailability of the said electronic filing platforms";
    - "When there is justifiable reason as may be determined by the Commissioner…";
    - "When the tax return is still unavailable in any of the aforementioned electronic filing platforms."

    Source: [BIR Digest of RMC 20-2026](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RMC%20No.%2020-2026%20Digest.pdf)

- **[Confirmed]** Under RMC 20-2026 Sec. IV, micro and small taxpayers may file BIR Form 1701-MS manually:
    - **Who may use it:** taxpayers engaged in business or practice of profession, including mixed-income earners, who are subject to a single income tax rate for business income (graduated, 8%, special/preferential, or exempt).
    - **Venue:** with tax payable, to any AAB, paying over the counter at any AAB; without tax payable, to any RDO.
    - **No refiling:** micro/small taxpayers who already e-filed 1701 or 1701A need not re-file on 1701-MS.
    - **No COR update:** those whose COR does not list 1701-MS may still file it manually without updating the COR.

    Source: [BDB Law advisory reproducing RMC 20-2026](https://www.bdblaw.com.ph/images/advisory/190_Advisory_RMC_No20-2026.pdf)

- **[Confirmed]** RMC 20-2026 Sec. VI (Penalties):
    - "No penalty shall be imposed for wrong venue."
    - No penalty applies to micro/small individual business taxpayers who filed 1701/1701A electronically or "filed and pay manually using BIR Form No. 1701-MS."

    Source: [BDB Law advisory reproducing RMC 20-2026](https://www.bdblaw.com.ph/images/advisory/190_Advisory_RMC_No20-2026.pdf)

- **[Needs verification – secondary only]** RMC 36-2026 (28 Apr 2026) released Offline eBIRForms v7.9.6.0, which adds BIR Form 1701-MS. RMC 37-2026 reportedly amended RMC 20-2026 to allow electronic filing of 1701-MS through that version. — [BIR Online Tools – eBIRForms v7.9.6.0 / RMC 36-2026](https://bir-online-tools.com/blog/posts/ebirforms-v7960-update-rmc-36-2026/); [Taxumo – New eBIRForms 7.9.6.0](https://www.taxumo.com/blog/ebirforms/); [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/bir-launches-enhanced-ebirforms-package-introduces-simplified-aitr-for-micro-small-taxpayers/)
- **[Confirmed – secondary]** RR 5-2015 (17 Mar 2015, amending RR 6-2014) set two penalties for taxpayers mandated to use eFPS or eBIRForms who did not:
    - P1,000 per return, under Sec. 250 NIRC;
    - in addition, a non-compliant filing was treated "tantamount to wrong venue filing pursuant to Section 248(A)(2)", carrying a 25% surcharge.

    Sources: [Grant Thornton (2015)](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/alerts-and-publications/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2015/penalties-for-failure-to-file-tax-returns-under-efps-and-ebirforms/); [lawyerly.ph – RR 5-2015 text](https://dev.lawyerly.ph/laws/67834). RMC 18-2015 waived these penalties for ITRs re-submitted electronically by 15 Jun 2015 (secondary, via search summary): [Roque Law](https://roquelaw.com.ph/?p=1541).

- **[Confirmed – secondary]** EOPT/RR 4-2024 removed the 25% surcharge for filing at the wrong venue. — [KPMG – Essential FAQs on filing and paying (RMC 87-2024)](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/insights/2025/01/essential-faqs-filing-tax-returns-and-paying-taxes-explained.html)

### Inferences

- **[Interpretation]** After EOPT, the 25% wrong-venue consequence of RR 5-2015 no longer applies. Whether the separate P1,000-per-return Sec. 250 penalty is still imposed on mandated e-filers who file on paper outside the allowed exceptions is not settled by the sources I could read. The app should still steer every user to e-filing and present paper filing only for the documented exceptions.
- **[Interpretation]** For 1701-MS, the app should offer two routes:
    - (a) e-file via eBIRForms v7.9.6.0 or later, per RMC 36/37-2026 (needs verification);
    - (b) print and file manually, at any AAB if there is tax payable or any RDO if not, per RMC 20-2026 (confirmed for CY2025).

### Gaps

- The full text of RMC 37-2026 was not retrieved, so its exact amendment to the manual 1701-MS route is unconfirmed.
- No source said whether the 1701-MS manual option continues for CY2026 returns (filed in 2027). This depends on next year's AITR RMC. **[Needs verification]**

## 3. What did EOPT (RA 11976) and RR 4-2024 change about where and how returns are filed and paid?

### Takeaway

EOPT/RR 4-2024 introduced "file and pay anywhere":

- returns are filed electronically by default;
- payment may be made through any AAB, any RCO, or an e-payment channel, not only within the taxpayer's RDO;
- the 25% wrong-venue surcharge was removed.

Late filers still go to their registered RDO for the penalty computation.

### Cited Findings

- **[Confirmed – secondary summary of RMC 87-2024]** RMC 87-2024, the FAQs implementing Sec. 3 of RR 4-2024, makes these points:
    - all tax returns must be filed electronically;
    - taxpayers enrolled in eFPS keep using eFPS, and may use eBIRForms if eFPS is unavailable;
    - mandated taxpayers not yet enrolled may use eBIRForms until approved.

    Source: [KPMG – Essential FAQs](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/insights/2025/01/essential-faqs-filing-tax-returns-and-paying-taxes-explained.html)

- **[Confirmed – secondary]** RMC 87-2024 manual filing rules:
    - Manual filing is allowed only where "the electronic filing platforms are non-operational", the form is not yet on any e-platform, or the CIR allows it for justifiable reasons.
    - Manual filers may file and pay at any RCO or AAB.
    - RCOs accept cash only up to Php20,000. There is no limit on checks.
    - Late-filing penalties "must be done at their registered RDO."

    Sources: [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/insights/2025/01/essential-faqs-filing-tax-returns-and-paying-taxes-explained.html); [Grant Thornton – FAQs on RR 4-2024](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2024/faqs-relative-to-the-filing-of-tax-returns-and-payment-of-taxes-pursuant-to-rr-no-4-2024-implementing-the-provisions-of-republic-act-no-11976-otherwise-known-as-eopt-act/); [PwC Tax Alert No. 38 (RMC 87-2024)](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/tax-alerts/2024/tax-alert-38.html) (403 on fetch; content from search snippet)

- **[Confirmed – secondary]** RR 4-2024 is dated 22 March 2024. It allows manual filing "in case of the unavailability of electronic platforms." — [PwC Tax Alert No. 22](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/tax-alerts/2024/tax-alert-22.html) (search snippet; page returned 403)
- **[Confirmed]** RMC 20-2026 restates EOPT payment practice. Payment "must be settled electronically" through eFPS or the ePayment gateways. Over-the-counter payment at any AAB is allowed in three cases:
    - the return was e-filed via eBIRForms;
    - the taxpayer's eFPS-AAB enrollment is pending;
    - eFPS or the eFPS-AAB is down.

    Source: [BIR Digest of RMC 20-2026](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RMC%20No.%2020-2026%20Digest.pdf)

- **[Confirmed]** The RDO eLounge is open to all taxpayers regardless of jurisdiction for e-filing help. Priority goes to:
    - senior citizens and PWDs;
    - certain employees;
    - taxpayers without internet.

    Practitioners are limited to 3 transactions per day or 1 hour, whichever is shorter (RMO 39-2024). Source: [BIR Digest of RMC 20-2026](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RMC%20No.%2020-2026%20Digest.pdf)

- **[Confirmed – secondary]** Malaya (Sept 2025) reported that the BIR lets taxpayers file AITRs anywhere without wrong-venue penalties. — [Malaya – File AITR anywhere sans penalties](https://malaya.com.ph/business-news/file-aitr-anywhere-sans-penalties-bir)

### Inferences

- **[Interpretation]** For the app, "where to pay" is not tied to the user's RDO. Any AAB branch nationwide, or any ePay gateway, is acceptable. The user's RDO still matters for late-filing penalty computation and for manual submission of attachments.

### Gaps

- The verbatim text of RR 4-2024 Sec. 3 was not retrieved (bir.gov.ph and PwC returned 403).

## 4. Can a taxpayer print a BIR PDF form, fill it, and file it on paper?

### Takeaway

Yes, but only where manual filing is itself permitted (Section 2). Historically, BIR issuances expressly accepted downloadable PDF forms printed on A4 bond paper, and computer-generated forms meeting set requirements, for manual filing. They did not have to come from eBIRForms. A printed PDF is not a substitute for e-filing when the taxpayer is required to e-file and the return is available in eBIRForms or eFPS.

### Cited Findings

- **[Confirmed – prior rule, 2018]** RMC 24-2018 directed RDOs and AABs to accept officially printed forms, photocopies, downloadable forms from the BIR website printed on A4 bond paper, and computer-generated returns in lieu of officially printed forms, if requirements are met. — [RMC 24-2018 (PwC copy)](https://pwc.com/ph/en/tax-alerts/assets/2018/RMC%20No%2024-2018.pdf) (search-snippet content)
- **[Confirmed – prior rule, 2020–2021]** Where a revised form was not yet in eBIRForms, "manual filers shall download the PDF version of the form, print the form, and completely fill out the applicable fields." That was RMC 13-2020. A 2021 circular said manual and eBIRForms filers must do the same "otherwise shall be subjected to penalties" under Sec. 250. — [PwC Tax Alert No. 10 (RMC 13-2020)](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/tax-alerts/2020/tax-alert-10.html); [PwC Tax Alert No. 54 (Dec 2021)](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/tax-alerts/2021-tax-alerts/tax-alert-54.html); [KPMG RMC 119-2021](https://kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ph/pdf/InTAX/2021/RMC%20No.%20119-2021.pdf)
- **[Confirmed]** RMC 20-2026 treats 1701-MS as a manually filed return for CY2025. The micro/small taxpayer prepares the BIR form outside eBIRForms and files it at an AAB or RDO. — [BDB Law advisory reproducing RMC 20-2026](https://www.bdblaw.com.ph/images/advisory/190_Advisory_RMC_No20-2026.pdf)
- **[Confirmed – prior practice]** Under older eBIRForms procedures ("eFile and manually pay"), the taxpayer brought a printed copy of the eBIRForms-generated return plus the email confirmation to the AAB. RMC 20-2026 now says the screenshot of the eBIRForms submission pop-up "may be presented to the AAB when paying." — [BIR job aid (SlideShare)](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/job-aid-for-taxpayers-how-to-use-the-e-birforms-package/46887707); [BIR Digest of RMC 20-2026](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RMC%20No.%2020-2026%20Digest.pdf)

### Inferences

- **[Interpretation]** For the app's generated output, two cases apply:
    - **A printed, filled copy of the BIR's official PDF** is acceptable on paper for 1701-MS (micro/small), for any return not yet on eBIRForms/eFPS, during an advisory-declared outage, or with CIR authorization.
    - **For everything else**, the PDF is a worksheet or reference copy. The user should re-key the figures into Offline eBIRForms, or file through a certified TSP, and keep the TRRC/FRN or email confirmation as proof of filing.
- **[Interpretation]** The app should use the BIR's own current PDF version of each form, not a redrawn layout, to fit the "downloadable form printed on A4" acceptance language.

### Gaps

- I found no 2024–2026 issuance that restates the RMC 24-2018 acceptance of downloaded or computer-generated forms. Whether RDOs and AABs still accept a third-party-filled BIR PDF in 2026 is **[Needs verification]**, ideally with an RDO.

## 5. Current filing and payment channels and system names (eBIRForms, eFPS, TSPs, ePay, ORUS, eAFS, eONETT, any new portal)

### Takeaway

As of 2026, filing runs through eFPS, Offline eBIRForms (v7.9.5 per RMC 20-2026; v7.9.6.0 released April 2026), and BIR-certified TSPs. Payment runs through eFPS-AABs, any AAB over the counter, RCOs, and the ePay gateways named below. I found no reliable announcement of a portal replacing eFPS or eBIRForms.

### Cited Findings

- **[Confirmed]** The ePayment gateways named in RMC 20-2026 are:
    - **LBP Link.BizPortal**: for LBP ATM holders, BancNet ATM/debit/prepaid holders, and PESONet users with AUB, BPI, PSBank, RCBC, Robinsons Bank or UnionBank.
    - **UnionBank Online / The Portal**: for UBP account holders, or via InstaPay using UPAY for non-account holders.
    - **DBP PayTax Online**: for Visa/MasterCard credit cards and BancNet ATM/debit cards, and through partner TSPs:
        - MyEG, accepting cards or e-wallets (GCash, Maya, GrabPay, ShopeePay);
        - the Maya mobile app.

    Annex B of the RMC covers ePay procedures. Source: [BIR Digest of RMC 20-2026](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RMC%20No.%2020-2026%20Digest.pdf)

- **[Confirmed]** Proof of filing:
    - FRN (Filing Reference Number) for eFPS;
    - TRRC (Tax Return Receipt Confirmation) for eBIRForms;
    - for eBIRForms, a screenshot of the pop-up confirming the email was sent is advised as backup proof.

    Source: [BDB Law advisory reproducing RMC 20-2026, Sec. V](https://www.bdblaw.com.ph/images/advisory/190_Advisory_RMC_No20-2026.pdf)

- **[Needs verification – secondary]** GCash has been described both as fee-free for BIR payments and as limited to payments under Php10,000. The sources conflict. — [Moneymax – BIR online payment](https://www.moneymax.ph/government-services/blog/bir-online-payment); [FilipiKnow](https://filipiknow.net/how-to-pay-bir-tax/)

### Inferences

- **[Interpretation]** The app's "how to file" text for a non-eFPS individual should read:

## 6. Electronic Invoicing System (EIS): coverage and timing; relevance to small taxpayers

### Takeaway

An enhanced EIS was launched in late October 2026 under a BIR circular dated 22 Sept 2026. Mandatory coverage is:

- taxpayers under the Large Taxpayers Service;
- large taxpayers under the EOPT framework;
- small, medium and large taxpayers engaged in e-commerce or internet transactions;
- taxpayers using CAS/CBA.

They must e-invoice on or before 31 Dec 2026. Micro taxpayers are not covered. A non-e-commerce small professional using manual books is outside mandatory coverage.

### Cited Findings

- **[Confirmed – government news agency]** PNA (27 Oct 2026) reported:
    - The circular dated 22 Sept 2026 covers "small, medium and large taxpayers engaged in e-commerce or internet transactions", "taxpayers under the Large Taxpayers Service", "large taxpayers under the Ease of Paying Taxes framework", and "taxpayers using a computerized accounting system and computerized book of accounts".
    - Covered taxpayers must issue e-invoices "on or before Dec. 31, 2026."
    - "Micro taxpayers are not covered by the mandatory electronic invoicing requirement."
    - The legal basis is Secs. 237 and 237-A NIRC as amended by TRAIN (RA 10963), which mandated e-invoicing within five years.
    - Separately, online sellers and content creators no longer need to display the BIR Registration Seal Badge from 31 Oct 2026.

    Source: [PNA – BIR launches enhanced electronic invoicing](https://www.pna.gov.ph/article/bir-launches-enhanced-electronic-invoicing)

- **[Needs verification – secondary, conflicting]** RR 26-2025 reportedly moved the compliance date to 31 Dec 2026. Another source describes a March 2026 rollout start for priority groups. — [Taxify – BIR mandatory e-invoicing 2026](https://taxify.ph/blog/bir-mandatory-e-invoicing-2026-guide/); [RTC Suite – EIS by 2026](https://rtcsuite.com/bir-e-invoicing-philippines-eis-by-2026-a-comprehensive-guide-to-scope-stages-and-technical-compliance/); [Sprout – BIR Online Guide 2026](https://sprout.ph/articles/bir-online-einvoicing-filing-payments/)

### Inferences

- **[Interpretation]** The app should flag EIS only for users who sell through e-commerce or internet channels, use CAS/CBA, or are large taxpayers. It should explicitly tell micro taxpayers they are not covered.
- **[Interpretation]** The PNA article cites TRAIN's Sec. 237-A rather than EOPT. The circular's own number was not given.

### Gaps

## 7. Attachments: eAFS submission of SAWT, 2307s and financial statements, and deadlines

### Takeaway

Attachments to the AITR go through eAFS. This includes 2307s, AFS (with a CPA certificate where required), SAWT and alphalist acknowledgments, and proofs of credits and payment. They are due within 15 days from the filing deadline, or within 15 days from actual filing if the return is late. Manual submission to the taxpayer's RDO is allowed only when eAFS is down under an advisory. This rule also applies to manual 1701-MS filers.

### Cited Findings

- **[Confirmed]** RMC 20-2026 Sec. V sets the deadline. Covered filers are "eBIRForms/eFPS/TSP Filers and Manual Filers (for BIR Form No. 1701-MS)". Attachments are due "Within 15 days from deadline of filing of return or Within 15 days from filing of the return (in case of late filing)". Online submission is through eAFS. Manual submission to the RDO with jurisdiction is allowed "in case of system unavailability with a duly released advisory." The eAFS-generated TRN/Confirmation Receipt is the proof of submission and replaces the "Received" stamp, including for SEC purposes. — [BDB Law advisory reproducing RMC 20-2026](https://www.bdblaw.com.ph/images/advisory/190_Advisory_RMC_No20-2026.pdf)
- **[Confirmed]** The required attachments listed in RMC 20-2026 are:
    - FRN or TRRC
    - Proof of payment
    - Certificate of independent CPA (BIR-accredited)
    - Unaudited or audited FS, notes, and Statement of Management Responsibility
    - BIR Form 2307
    - BIR Form 1606
    - BIR Form 2304
    - BIR Form 2316
    - System-generated acknowledgment or validation report of the Summary Alphalist of Withholding Taxes (SAWT) submitted through esubmission@bir.gov.ph
    - Approved Tax Debit Memo
    - Proofs of foreign tax credits, prior-year excess credits and other credits
    - BIR Form 1709 (related-party transactions)

    Source: [BDB Law advisory reproducing RMC 20-2026](https://www.bdblaw.com.ph/images/advisory/190_Advisory_RMC_No20-2026.pdf)

- **[Confirmed – secondary]** RMC 87-2024 says attachments go through eAFS or the eSubmission facility, depending on the document type. — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/insights/2025/01/essential-faqs-filing-tax-returns-and-paying-taxes-explained.html)
- **[Needs verification – conflicting]** For CY2025 returns, the deadline was extended:
    - RMC 39-2026 reportedly set 15 May 2026 for attachments to returns filed 15–16 April or 15 May;
    - another commentary cites RMC 46-2026 extending to 25 May 2026 for system-affected taxpayers.

    Sources: [batasnatin – RMC 39-2026](https://batasnatin.com/laws/rmc-no-39-2026-clarification-on-the-deadline-for-the-submission-of-required-a) (403 on fetch); [Respicio – eAFS deadline commentary](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/bir-eafs-attachment-upload-deadline-and-late-submission-penalties-philippines)

### Inferences

- **[Interpretation]** The app should compute the attachment deadline as filing deadline + 15 days. For late filers, it should use actual filing date + 15 days. It should warn that BIR often issues year-specific extensions.
- **[Interpretation]** For quarterly returns (1701Q, 2550Q, 2551Q, 1601-EQ), the SAWT and QAP are submitted through the eSubmission channel (esubmission@bir.gov.ph), not eAFS. This is based on the SAWT entry above; I did not confirm per-return timing. **[Needs verification]**

## 8. Per-form channel map for the app's returns (synthesis)

### Takeaway

For every return the app prepares, the default instruction for a non-eFPS individual or small business is: e-file through Offline eBIRForms (or a certified TSP), then pay electronically or over the counter at any AAB. Use eFPS only if enrolled. The only routine paper route confirmed for 2026 is 1701-MS. The forms below are confirmed in eFPS/eBIRForms only where a source names them.

### Cited Findings

- **[Confirmed]** 1701 and 1701A are available in eFPS and in Offline eBIRForms (1701v2018 and 1701A). 1702-RT (1702RTv2018C) is also in both. — [BIR Digest of RMC 20-2026](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RMC%20No.%2020-2026%20Digest.pdf)
- **[Confirmed]** 1701-MS is filed manually, at any AAB with payment or any RDO with no payment, per RMC 20-2026 for CY2025. — [BDB Law advisory](https://www.bdblaw.com.ph/images/advisory/190_Advisory_RMC_No20-2026.pdf). It became e-fileable via eBIRForms v7.9.6.0 under RMC 36-2026/37-2026 **[Needs verification]**: [BIR Online Tools](https://bir-online-tools.com/blog/posts/ebirforms-v7960-update-rmc-36-2026/).
- **[Needs verification – secondary]** eBIRForms v7.9.6.0 includes 0619-E, 1601-EQ, 1702Q, 2000-OT and 2551Q, since bug fixes or ATC updates for these forms are listed in that release. — [BIR Online Tools](https://bir-online-tools.com/blog/posts/ebirforms-v7960-update-rmc-36-2026/)
- **[Confirmed – prior]** The eBIRForms package covered about 36 BIR forms, including income tax, VAT, withholding and DST forms. — [Inquirer (2014)](https://business.inquirer.net/188934/bir-to-strictly-implement-e-filing-system)
