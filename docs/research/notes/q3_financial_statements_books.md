# BIR rules on financial statements attached to annual ITRs and on books of accounts (individuals and small corporations)

Research date: 2026-11-02. Labels: **Confirmed** = issuance plus section/URL seen in a primary text or a reputable firm alert quoting it; **Interpretation** = my reading of confirmed text; **Needs verification** = secondary source only, partial text, or conflicting sources.

Source caveat: bir.gov.ph pages and several aggregator pages (batasnatin.com, pwc.com/ph) returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool. Primary text was read from lawphil.net (RA 10963, RA 11976) and the Supreme Court e-Library (RR 9-2009). The rest comes from P&A Grant Thornton, KPMG and other firm alerts.

## 1. NIRC Sec. 232(A) as amended by TRAIN: CPA audit threshold and what applies at or below it

### Takeaway

TRAIN (RA 10963, Sec. 71) amended Sec. 232(A). Books must be audited yearly by an independent CPA when **gross ANNUAL sales, earnings, receipts or output exceed ₱3,000,000**. Before TRAIN the test was gross _quarterly_ sales above ₱150,000. At or below ₱3M no CPA audit is required. The simplified-bookkeeping proviso (quarterly sales not over ₱50,000 in the 1997 text) appears to survive, but I could not see its post-TRAIN wording.

### Cited Findings

- **Confirmed.** RA 10963 lists Sec. 232 among the NIRC sections it amends. In the Act text, Sec. 71 amends Sec. 232(A) to read in part: those "whose gross annual sales, earnings, receipts or output exceed Three million pesos (₱3,000,000)" "shall have their books of accounts audited and examined yearly by independent Certified Public Accountants". The excerpt I could read ended with "x x x", so the rest of 232(A), and all of 232(B), were not visible. — [RA 10963, lawphil.net](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2017/ra_10963_2017.html)
- **Confirmed (pre-TRAIN baseline).** The 1997 NIRC Sec. 232(A) text said those "whose quarterly sales, earnings, receipts, or output do not exceed Fifty thousand pesos (P50,000) shall keep and use simplified set of bookkeeping records duly authorized by the Secretary of Finance". — [ChanRobles, NIRC Title IX](https://www.chanrobles.com/legal6title37.htm)
- **Confirmed (historical context).** A June 2017 P&A Grant Thornton article on the TRAIN _bill_ said the House version would raise the simplified-bookkeeping threshold from ₱200,000 per year to ₱1M per year, and the CPA-audit threshold from ₱600,000 to ₱3M. That was the House bill. The enacted text kept the ₱3M audit figure, but I could not confirm that the simplified-bookkeeping change was enacted. — [P&A Grant Thornton, "Are you ready for simplified bookkeeping and e-Invoicing?" (06 Jun 2017)](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/lets-talk-tax/are-you-ready-for-simplified-bookkeeping-and-e-invoicing/)
- **Confirmed (secondary).** Simplified bookkeeping under RR V-1 consists of: a record of daily sales and cash receipts; a record of daily purchases, expenses and cash disbursements; a record of the summary of transactions; and yearly statements of net worth and operations. — [P&A Grant Thornton (2017)](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/lets-talk-tax/are-you-ready-for-simplified-bookkeeping-and-e-invoicing/)
- **Confirmed (secondary).** Firm and practitioner guides consistently say AFS with an independent CPA's report and certificate are attachments to the annual ITR when gross annual sales/receipts exceed ₱3,000,000. The attachment list includes a "CPA Certificate (if gross annual sales, earnings, receipts, or output exceed Php 3,000,000.00)" alongside the "Account Information Form and/or financial statements". — [Aureada Law, 2026 guide to AFS](https://www.aureadalaw.com/post/2026-guide-to-audited-financial-statements-for-companies-and-taxpayers); [Filepino, who must submit AFS](https://www.filepino.com/who-is-required-to-submit-an-audited-financial-statement-afs/)
- **Needs verification (conflict).** One search snippet still quoted the old "gross quarterly sales ... exceed PHP150,000" test, which is pre-TRAIN and outdated. A 2026 practitioner source says corporations with total assets or liabilities at or below ₱3M may submit unaudited FS with a sworn statement of management responsibility (SMR). That is an SEC filing rule tied to assets, not the NIRC Sec. 232 sales test. — [Respicio, SMR for eAFS](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/when-is-an-smr-required-for-eafs-submission)

### Inferences

- **Interpretation.** App rule: if gross annual sales/receipts > ₱3,000,000 → books audited by an independent CPA, and AFS plus auditor's report attached to the AITR (via eAFS). If ≤ ₱3,000,000 → no CPA audit required by the NIRC. A taxpayer on itemized deductions still attaches unaudited FS, or an Account Information Form (AIF) / FS signed by the taxpayer (see Q2). Use "exceeds" (strictly greater than), as the statute says.
- **Interpretation.** Corporations (SEC-registered) have a separate SEC AFS obligation (Revised Corporation Code / SEC rules) that does not depend on the ₱3M BIR test. A small corporation should not be told "no audit needed" based on the BIR rule alone. This is outside scope; flag it for CPA review.
- **Interpretation.** The ₱3M audit threshold matches the VAT threshold and the 8% eligibility ceiling, so an 8%-eligible individual is, by definition, below the CPA-audit threshold.

### Gaps

- Full post-TRAIN text of Sec. 232(A) after the audit proviso (whether the ₱50,000 quarterly simplified-bookkeeping proviso was retained or changed), and the text of Sec. 232(B) (persons whose books are not kept in a native language). The lawphil excerpt was truncated. Check the BIR's consolidated NIRC (bir.gov.ph returned 403).
- Whether any RR currently implements "simplified bookkeeping" beyond RR V-1. None was found.

## 2. Must individuals attach FS to 1701/1701A? 8% taxpayers? (RR 8-2018, form guidelines)

### Takeaway

Individuals who elect **OSD or the 8% rate** (Form 1701A) are **not required to submit financial statements** (RMC 17-2019; for OSD the rule goes back to RR 16-2008). Individuals on **graduated rates with itemized deductions** (Form 1701), and mixed-income earners, attach FS: audited by an independent CPA if gross annual sales/receipts exceed ₱3M, otherwise unaudited FS or an Account Information Form.

### Cited Findings

- **Confirmed.** RMC 17-2019 introduced BIR Form 1701A for individuals earning purely from business/profession who elect OSD or the 8% rate, and says "such individuals who elected OSD or 8% flat income tax rate are no longer required to submit financial statements." Individuals on itemized deductions, and those with both compensation and business income, "shall still use ... BIR Form 1701". — [P&A Grant Thornton Tax Alert on RMC 17-2019 (08 Feb 2019)](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/alerts-and-publications/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2019/new-annual-income-tax-return-for-individuals-availing-osd-and-8-income-tax-rate/); [KPMG copy of RMC 17-2019](https://home.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ph/pdf/InTAX/2019/RMC%20No%2017-2019.pdf)
- **Confirmed (secondary).** RR 16-2008 (implementing the OSD under RA 9504) provides that individuals claiming OSD are not required to submit financial statements but must keep records of gross sales or receipts. — [Respicio, OSD and income statement requirement](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/optional-standard-deduction-and-income-statement-requirement); [jur.ph summary of RR 16-2008](https://jur.ph/law/facts/implementing-optional-standard-deduction-tax-code-amendment)
- **Needs verification (conflict).** A KPMG-hosted annex of AITR filing guidelines for the 1701A (Jan 2018) appears to list audited or unaudited FS and a CPA certificate among the requirements under the 8% entry. This conflicts with RMC 17-2019 and may be a generic attachment list or a different tax year. I did not open the PDF. — [KPMG Annex A 1701A Jan 2018](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ph/pdf/InTAX/2019/Annex%20A%20-%201701A%20Jan%202018.pdf); [KPMG Annex A Filing Guidelines AITR 2022](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ph/pdf/InTAX/2023/Annex%20A%20-%20Filing%20Guidelines%20AITR_2022_Final.pdf)
- **Confirmed (secondary).** Form 1701 attachments for business/professional income include "Account Information Form and/or financial statements" and, above ₱3M gross annual sales, the independent CPA certificate and AFS. — [Filepino](https://www.filepino.com/who-is-required-to-submit-an-audited-financial-statement-afs/); [P&A Grant Thornton, 1701 Jan 2018 enclosures (PDF)](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/globalassets/1.-member-firms/philippines/tax-alerts/2019/03.22.2019/1701-jan-2018-encs.-final.pdf)

### Inferences

- **Interpretation.** App rule matrix (individuals):
    - 8% option (1701A): no FS attachment. Attach BIR Form 2307 certificates if any. Books must still be kept and registered (Q3).
    - Graduated + OSD (1701A): no FS attachment. Keep records of gross sales/receipts.
    - Graduated + itemized (1701): attach FS. AFS with independent CPA report if gross annual sales/receipts > ₱3M; otherwise unaudited FS or AIF (balance sheet and income statement).
    - Mixed income on itemized (1701): same as above for the business portion.
- **Interpretation.** Because OSD/8% filers attach no FS, the app need not produce a balance sheet for them. It should still keep the sales/receipts records the books require.

### Gaps

- I could not read RR 8-2018's own text (bir.gov.ph 403), so I cannot quote its section on FS for 8% filers. RMC 17-2019 is the confirmed authority used above.
- The exact current 1701 (Jan 2018 or later revision) guideline wording on "AIF vs FS" for sub-₱3M itemized filers needs checking against the form PDF on bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph.

## 3. Which books a non-VAT professional/sole proprietor must register; manual vs loose-leaf vs computerized

### Takeaway

Sec. 232(A) requires every person liable for internal revenue taxes to keep "a journal and a ledger or their equivalents". Simplified books apply to very small taxpayers. Under EOPT-era rules (RR 7-2024, RMC 65-2025) a taxpayer picks **one** format: manual, loose-leaf (needs a Permit to Use), or computerized (needs an Acknowledgement Certificate). Those choosing loose-leaf or computerized need not also register manual books.

### Cited Findings

- **Confirmed (statute baseline).** The 1997 NIRC Sec. 232(A) provides that all persons required to pay internal revenue taxes "shall keep a journal and a ledger or their equivalents", subject to the simplified-bookkeeping proviso. — [ChanRobles, NIRC Title IX](https://www.chanrobles.com/legal6title37.htm)
- **Confirmed (secondary).** RMC 65-2025: new and existing registrants may choose Manual Books, Loose-leaf Books (LLBA) or Computerized Books (CBA). "Those opting for LLBA or CBA are not required to register Manual Books of Accounts." LLBA requires a Permit to Use (PTU). CBA/CAS requires an Acknowledgement Certificate (AC). These "may only be issued after the taxpayer has been assigned a TIN", so they cannot be processed together with TIN issuance. — [P&A Grant Thornton, "BIR clarifies bookkeeping options for new business registrants" (16 Jul 2025)](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/tax-notes/bir-clarifies-bookkeeping-options-for-new-business-registrants/); [KPMG news flash (Jul 2025)](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2025/07/philippines-guidance-books-accounts-vat-zero-rate-documentation.html)
- **Confirmed (secondary).** RR 7-2024 (EOPT implementing rules on registration and invoicing): register manual books; register loose-leaf and CAS books "within the prescribed period". Computer-aided invoices need a permit to use loose-leaf invoices plus an ATP. CAS needs an Acknowledgement Certificate. — [P&A Grant Thornton, EOPT updates on preservation of books and registration](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/tax-notes/eopt-is-here-updates-on-the-preservation-of-book-of-accounts-and-changes-in-taxpayer-registration/)
- **Needs verification.** Practitioner guides list the typical manual books for a non-VAT service provider or professional as a General Journal, General Ledger, Cash Receipts Journal and Cash Disbursements Journal. A VAT or goods seller adds Sales and Purchase journals or subsidiary books. I found no BIR issuance in this session listing these by taxpayer type. — [Triple i Consulting, what are books of account](https://www.tripleiconsulting.com/what-are-books-account-philippines/); [FilipiKnow, books of accounts BIR](https://filipiknow.net/books-of-accounts-bir/)

### Inferences

- **Interpretation.** Default recommendation for a non-VAT professional on 8%: register a journal and a ledger (manual, or loose-leaf/CBA if preferred). Many RDOs also expect cash receipts and disbursements journals. Treat the exact set as "confirm with your RDO/CPA".
- **Interpretation.** Choosing loose-leaf or computerized books after TIN issuance needs a separate PTU/AC step. The app should warn that recording must not start in an unregistered format.

### Gaps

- No primary BIR list of mandatory books by taxpayer class was retrieved (RMO 29-2002 and Form 1900 guidelines were not read).

## 4. Loose-leaf books: permit, binding and registration deadlines

### Takeaway

Loose-leaf books need a Permit to Use (via BIR Form 1900, now processed through ORUS). They must be **permanently bound and registered within 15 days after the end of each taxable year** (or within 15 days of closing the business, if earlier). Since 2025–2026, registration goes **online via ORUS**, and a QR code is affixed to the first page. In both recent seasons the BIR extended the January 15 deadline to January 31.

### Cited Findings

- **Needs verification (consistent secondary sources).** Permanently bound loose-leaf books are registered within 15 days after the end of the taxable year or within 15 days of closing the business, whichever is earlier, unless the BIR extends it on a request filed before the deadline. Computerized books get 30 days. Sources attribute this to RMC 29-2019. — [ASEAN Briefing](https://www.aseanbriefing.com/news/how-do-companies-register-their-books-of-accounts-with-the-bir-in-the-philippines/); [Aureada Law, 2026 loose-leaf binding deadlines](https://www.aureadalaw.com/post/bir-philippines-loose-leaf-books-binding-registration-deadlines-for-2026); [PwC, "Wrapping up the year right"](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/taxwise-or-otherwise/2019-taxwise-or-otherwise/wrapping-up-the-year-right.html)
- **Confirmed.** RMC 6-2025 (issued 16 Jan 2025) extended the TY2024 registration deadlines for permanently bound loose-leaf books/invoices and computerized books to **31 Jan 2025** and **17 Feb 2025** respectively, because of ORUS log-in problems. — [P&A Grant Thornton Tax Alert on RMC 6-2025](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2024/registration-of-permanently-bound-loose-leaf-books-of-accounts-invoices-and-computerized-books-of-accounts-and-other-accounting-records-extended-until-january-31-2025-and-february-17-2025-respectively/)
- **Confirmed.** RMC 4-2026 (issued 15 Jan 2026, TY2025): taxpayers using permanently bound loose-leaf or computerized books "must register their books of accounts online strictly via ORUS". The QR code is "affixed to the first page of permanently bound loose-leaf books". For computerized books it is printed and kept on file. Manual stamping at the RDO is allowed only during an official ORUS-downtime advisory or with a screenshot of the error. Loose-leaf invoices/receipts and other records are still processed manually at the RDO. The deadlines were extended. — [P&A Grant Thornton Tax Alert on RMC 4-2026](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2025/clarification-on-the-mandatory-orus-registration-of-looseleaf-and-computerized-books-and-extension-of-deadlines/)
- **Needs verification.** The RMC 4-2026 extended dates were reported as 31 Jan 2026 (loose-leaf) and 17 Feb 2026 (computerized) by a secondary search summary. The GT alert did not state the dates. — [Aureada Law](https://www.aureadalaw.com/post/bir-philippines-loose-leaf-books-binding-registration-deadlines-for-2026)

### Inferences

- **Interpretation.** App reminders: Jan 15 (loose-leaf bound books via ORUS) and Jan 30 (computerized books via ORUS) for calendar-year taxpayers, with a check for BIR extension RMCs each January. On business closure, the deadline is 15 or 30 days from closure if earlier.

### Gaps

- Primary text of RMC 29-2019, RR 7-2024's registration-deadline section and the Form 1900 guidelines was not read (bir.gov.ph 403). The 15/30-day rules rest on consistent firm and practitioner sources.

## 5. Computerized books / CAS: is there a mandatory threshold? RR 9-2009, RMO 29-2002, RR 7-2024

### Takeaway

CAS is **mandatory only for Large Taxpayers** (RR 9-2009, by classification under RR 1-98, not a sales threshold). For everyone else it is optional. Registration moved from a "Permit to Use" to an **Acknowledgement Certificate** (RMC 5-2021 / RMO 9-2021, kept under RR 7-2024). E-invoicing and e-sales reporting (Sec. 237/237-A) are a separate, invoice-side obligation.

### Cited Findings

- **Confirmed.** RR 9-2009 (23 Dec 2009, "Maintenance, Retention, and Submission of Electronic Records") Sec. 1.4 and 4.1 make CAS mandatory for "Large Taxpayers classified under Revenue Regulations No. 1-98". Sec. 4.2 required Large Taxpayers on manual books to register CAS "not later than December 31, 2009". Sec. 5.4.4 lists required fields for the General Ledger/Journal and the sales, purchase and inventory books. Sec. 10.1 says records are preserved per NIRC Sec. 235. Sec. 11.1 requires all computerized books and systems to be registered under RMO 29-2002. No peso threshold is stated. — [RR 9-2009, SC e-Library](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/48854)
- **Confirmed (secondary).** RMO 9-2021 covers both Large and Non-Large Taxpayers "who opted to use CAS, CBA and components". RMC 5-2021 streamlined registration using BIR Form 1900, and an Acknowledgement Certificate replaced the Permit to Use. — [KPMG copy of RMO 9-2021](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ph/pdf/InTAX/2021/RMO%20No.%209-2021.pdf); [P&A Grant Thornton, streamlined CAS/CBA registration (2021)](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/alerts-and-publications/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2021/streamlined-process-on-the-application-for-registration-of-cascba/)
- **Confirmed.** TRAIN Sec. 73 (amending NIRC Sec. 237(A)) requires electronic invoicing, within 5 years of effectivity, for Large Taxpayers, exporters and e-commerce taxpayers. TRAIN Sec. 74 (new Sec. 237-A) requires those taxpayers to report sales data electronically via POS systems. — [RA 10963, lawphil.net](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2017/ra_10963_2017.html)
- **Needs verification (likely wrong).** One commercial summary says CAS applies if annual gross sales exceed ₱3M or if a POS/CRM is used. No issuance supports this, and it conflicts with RR 9-2009. — [ClearTax PH, CAS registration](https://www.cleartax.com/ph/bir-cas-registration)

### Inferences

- **Interpretation.** For the app's audience (individuals and small corporations, non-LT), CAS is optional. If the user's own app ledger is used as their "books", it would need CBA/CAS registration (Acknowledgement Certificate). Otherwise the user must print to registered manual or loose-leaf books. The app should not present its data as BIR-registered books unless the user has registered it.

### Gaps

- RMO 29-2002 text and the current CAS/CBA guidelines under RR 7-2024 were not read directly.

## 6. Retention period (NIRC Sec. 235 as amended by EOPT)

### Takeaway

**5 years**, counted from the day after the return filing deadline, or from the actual filing date if the return was filed late, for the taxable year of the last entry (RA 11976 Sec. 33 amending NIRC Sec. 235). Manual and loose-leaf books are kept as hard copies; computerized books as electronic copies. Records must be kept longer while a material protest or refund claim is pending. This replaced the pre-EOPT rule of up to 10 years.

### Cited Findings

- **Confirmed.** RA 11976 (EOPT Act, approved 5 Jan 2024) Sec. 33 amends Sec. 235: books "including the subsidiary books and other accounting records ... shall be preserved by them for a period of five (5) years reckoned from the day following the deadline in filing a return, or if filed after the deadline, from the date of filing a return, for the taxable year when the last entry was made". Income tax examination is limited to "only once in a taxable year", with exceptions. Sec. 51 sets effectivity at 15 days after publication. RA 11976 does not amend Secs. 232–234. — [RA 11976, lawphil.net](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2024/ra_11976_2024.html)
- **Confirmed (secondary).** RR 7-2024: manual and loose-leaf books are preserved in hard copy, and computerized books in electronic copy. Material records are kept "until the case is finally resolved" while a protest or refund claim is pending. The independent CPA keeps electronic copies of the audited FS and audit working papers for 5 years from the AITR due date or actual filing date, whichever is later. RR 7-2024 was posted on the BIR site on 12 Apr 2024 and takes effect 15 days after publication. — [P&A Grant Thornton, EOPT updates on preservation of books](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/tax-notes/eopt-is-here-updates-on-the-preservation-of-book-of-accounts-and-changes-in-taxpayer-registration/)
- **Confirmed (context).** Before EOPT, rules required retention "up to 10 years" (as of 2017). — [P&A Grant Thornton (2017)](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/lets-talk-tax/are-you-ready-for-simplified-bookkeeping-and-e-invoicing/)

### Inferences

- **Interpretation.** App rule: for each tax year, retain_until = (AITR deadline + 1 day, or the actual filing date if late) + 5 years. Example: for TY2025, if the deadline was moved to 15 May 2026 (RMC 30-2026), the clock runs from 16 May 2026 to about 15 May 2031. Extend while any protest or refund is open. Whether a deadline extension by RMC shifts the reckoning date is my interpretation; confirm with a CPA.

### Gaps

- RR 7-2024 issue date is reported inconsistently (22 Mar vs 11 Apr 2024). Its exact section numbers on preservation were not read from primary text.

## 7. eAFS: deadline for electronic submission of AFS and attachments

### Takeaway

Attachments to the AITR (AFS, ITR attachments) are uploaded through the BIR **eAFS** system **within 15 days after the AITR filing deadline**, or within 15 days after actual filing if the return was late. The BIR often extends this in practice: for TY2025 the AITR and attachments deadline was moved to 15 May 2026, with a further no-penalty eAFS window to 25 May 2026.

### Cited Findings

- **Needs verification (secondary).** Under RMC 20-2026, attachments are due within 15 days from the AITR deadline for on-time returns, or within 15 days from actual filing for late returns. Deadlines falling on a weekend move to the next working day. — [Respicio, AFS submission deadline rules](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/afs-submission-deadline-rules-for-the-bir-online-portal); [Respicio, eAFS weekend deadlines](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/eafs-filing-deadlines-when-the-due-date-falls-on-a-weekend)
- **Confirmed.** For TY2025, RMC 30-2026 moved the AITR deadline to 15 May 2026. RMC 46-2026 (18 May 2026) gave taxpayers who could not upload via eAFS by 15 May 2026, because of system issues, until **25 May 2026** "without the imposition of penalties". Taxpayers who received an email acknowledgement under the contingency procedure by 15 May are compliant. The extension "shall not be construed as an extension of the statutory deadline for the filing of the AITR". — [P&A Grant Thornton Tax Alert on RMC 46-2026 (25 May 2026)](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2026/extended-deadline-for-submission-of-2025-audited-financial-statements-and-other-required-attachments-to-the-annual-income-tax-return/); [KPMG Special InTAX May 2026](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/insights/2026/05/special-intax-may-2026-issue-1-volume-5.html)
- **Confirmed (secondary).** eAFS is the web platform for electronically submitting the attachments to a filed AITR. — [Respicio, how to file eAFS](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/how-to-file-eafs-audited-financial-statements-in-bir-online-system-philippines)
- **Confirmed (older baseline).** RMC 44-2021 is an earlier eAFS issuance. — [KPMG copy of RMC 44-2021](https://kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ph/pdf/InTAX/2021/RMC%20No.%2044-2021%20revised.pdf)

### Inferences

- **Interpretation.** App rule: attachments_due = AITR deadline + 15 days (e.g., 30 April for an ordinary 15 April deadline), rolled to the next working day. Show "check for BIR extension RMC" each season. 8%/OSD individuals have no FS to upload, but may still upload other attachments such as 2307s if eAFS submission applies to them.

### Gaps

- RMC 20-2026's primary text was not read. Its 15-day rule and whether it covers all individual filers (as against corporations or eFPS users) need verification. I found no confirmed rule on whether non-eFPS individual filers must use eAFS or may attach on paper at the RDO.
