# Philippine Expanded (Creditable) Withholding Tax for Self-Employed Individuals, and No-Payment (Nil) Return Obligations (as of Nov 2026)

Status labels used below: **Confirmed** (the issuance is named and the source supports it, but most sources are tax-firm summaries, not the BIR text itself), **Interpretation** (my reading of the sources), **Needs verification** (unsourced, conflicting, or possibly outdated).

Caveat on recency: nearly every source found dates from 2015 to 2024. I found no 2025 or 2026 issuance that changes these rules, but I also could not search for one thoroughly. The Ease of Paying Taxes Act (RA 11976, 2024) and its regulations (RR 4-2024 to RR 8-2024 range) may have changed details such as where returns are filed, when the duty to withhold arises, and penalties for micro and small taxpayers. Check these before relying on the notes for tax years 2024 to 2026.

## Q1. Nil returns: must 0619-E, 1601-EQ, 1604-E, 2551Q, 1701Q and 1702Q be filed when nothing is due?

### Takeaway

BIR guidance has consistently treated a registered tax type as needing a return every period. Business taxpayers file "no-payment" returns electronically (eBIRForms or eFPS). Zero-tax quarterly income and percentage tax returns are still filed and can be penalized if late. I found no source that clearly settles whether 1604-E is required for a year with zero withholding. No source covered 1702Q.

### Cited Findings

- **Confirmed (RMC 12-2015):** business taxpayers with no-payment returns who are required to use eBIRForms or eFPS "must electronically file returns". Manual filing at the RDO is allowed only for certain individuals: senior citizens or persons with disability, pure compensation earners whose tax was fully withheld, and substituted filers who choose to file. — [P&A Grant Thornton, Clarification on filing of no-payment returns (RMC 12-2015)](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/alerts-and-publications/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2015/clarification-on-filing-of-no-payment-returns/)
- **Confirmed (RMC 28-2017, 7 Apr 2017):** non-eFPS taxpayers with no tax due "should file their Income Tax Returns (ITR) through the eBIRForms package". Manual no-payment filing is allowed only for four groups: senior citizens or persons with disability; employees with two or more employers in the year; employees with one employer whose spouse cannot use substituted filing; and substituted filers who file anyway for loans, travel and similar needs. — [P&A Grant Thornton, Manual filing of no-payment returns (RMC 28-2017)](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/alerts-and-publications/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2017/manual-filing-of-no-payment-returns/)
- **Confirmed (KPMG 2018 summary of a TRAIN-era withholding issuance; the exact RMC number was not shown in the snippet):** "In cases wherein the manual filer has no withholding tax due for the month, the taxpayer is required to file 'No Payment' returns through the use of eBIRForms." This applies to the monthly 0619-E. — [KPMG Special InTAX, Sept 2018](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/home/insights/2018/09/special-intax-sept2018-issue1-vol1.html) (search snippet only; the page fetch returned only the KPMG homepage). A related search snippet says that once a taxpayer has filed through eBIRForms, it "shall continue to file its subsequent tax returns via the platform whether or not there are any tax payments due". — [KPMG Special InTAX, Feb 2021 (RMC 18-2021)](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/home/insights/2021/02/special-intax-february2021-issue1-volume1.html)
- **Confirmed (secondary source):** 0619-E is filed only in the first two months of each quarter (Jan, Feb, Apr, May, Jul, Aug, Oct, Nov). For the third month only the quarterly 1601-EQ is filed. — [Rappler Q&A](https://www.rappler.com/?p=235073)
- **Needs verification: 1601-EQ with zero withholding for the whole quarter.** No source found states directly that a no-payment 1601-EQ is required. — [search summary noting the gap; juan.tax 1601-EQ guide](https://juan.tax/blog/form-1601-eq-all-you-need-to-know)
- **Needs verification: 1604-E with zero withholding for the year.** 1604-E is filed "by a withholding agent who is required to withhold taxes on income payments", due March 1 of the following year. — [Roque Law, updated filing guidelines for 1604-C/1604-F/1604-E](https://roquelaw.com.ph/updated-filing-guidelines-for-bir-forms-1604c-1604f-and-1604e-submission-of-bir-form-2316-without-employee-signature-shall-be-accepted/). One practitioner commentary says that for years with no EWT payments, "some RDOs still expect" the return if the tax type is active, and practice varies. — [Respicio, Resolve BIR open cases for 1601-E and 1604-E](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/resolve-bir-open-cases-for-1601-e-and-1604-e-philippines). A forms aggregator gives a January 31 deadline, which conflicts with March 1. Treat it as unreliable. — [forms-legal.com](https://forms-legal.com/philippines/government/tax-forms/bir-form-1604e-philippines)
- **Confirmed (secondary sources): 1701Q and 2551Q with zero sales.** "The tax payable will be zero, but [the] return must be filed." A zero-tax quarterly return can still be penalized for late filing. An 8% taxpayer files only the 1701Q quarterly. A graduated-rate taxpayer files 1701Q and 2551Q. Filing one return does not cure a missed return of another tax type. — [FilePino, zero business transactions](https://www.filepino.com/zero-business-transactions-mandatory-bir-filing-taxes/); [Respicio, penalties for late quarterly filing with no declared income](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/penalties-for-late-quarterly-tax-filing-with-no-declared-income); [Lawyer-Philippines, freelancers without clients after registering a COR](https://www.lawyer-philippines.com/articles/do-freelancers-need-to-pay-taxes-without-clients-after-registering-a-bir-cor)
- **Penalties, Needs verification on amounts.** Commentary on RMO 7-2015 (revised schedule of compromise penalties) says failure to file starts at a ₱1,000 compromise for a first offense and rises for repeat offenses. A late no-payment return can still draw a compromise penalty. — [Respicio, compromise penalties for zero-tax-due professionals](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/compromise-penalties-for-zero-tax-due-professionals-under-3m-income-philippines); [Respicio, two-day late filing](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/bir-late-filing-penalties-for-a-two-day-delay-in-the-philippines); RMO 7-2015 text at [Lawyerly (RMO 7-2015)](https://dev.lawyerly.ph/laws/69577), not fetched.
- **Confirmed (RA 11976 EOPT, via commentary):** for micro and small taxpayers, the Sec. 248 surcharge is cut to 10% and the Sec. 249 interest is halved. RR 6-2024 implements this prospectively. Commentary notes that compromise penalties are separate from the basic tax, surcharge and interest, so the reduction does not obviously extend to the RMO 7-2015 schedule. — [Respicio](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/bir-late-filing-penalties-for-a-two-day-delay-in-the-philippines); [P&A Grant Thornton EOPT comparative summary](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/contentassets/2208a706d6c743e0a9a6c4f6d89d81b1/eopt-act-comparative-summary.pdf)

### Inferences

- **Interpretation:** a registered withholding agent (a 0619-E and 1601-EQ tax type on its COR) should file no-payment 0619-E returns for the first two months, and a no-payment 1601-EQ for the quarter. The general rule in RMC 12-2015 and RMC 28-2017 is that business no-payment returns are filed electronically, not skipped. Open-case tracking by the RDO is driven by registered tax types (Respicio). A reasonable engine default is to flag every registered period as "return due, ₱0" rather than "no return".
- **Interpretation:** the practical fix for a self-employed person who has no payees is to update registration (Form 1905) and drop the withholding tax types. Otherwise the BIR system keeps generating open cases.
- **Interpretation:** a 1701Q and a 2551Q (if not on 8%) are due every quarter while the person is registered, even with zero sales.

### Gaps

- No primary BIR text was fetched on whether a no-payment 1601-EQ or 1604-E is mandatory. The KPMG 2018 source covers the monthly no-payment remittance return only.
- No source covered **1702Q** with zero income for corporations. By analogy to the above it is expected to be filed, but that is unconfirmed.
- The RMO 7-2015 compromise schedule was not fetched. The ₱1,000 first-offense figure comes from secondary commentary, and the exact amount for no-tax-due returns is unconfirmed.
- Not checked: whether EOPT or RR 4-2024 changed penalty or open-case practice for no-payment withholding returns.

## Q2. ATC codes and rates (professional fees, rentals, contractors)

### Takeaway

Under RR 11-2018 as amended by RR 14-2018, professional, talent and commission fees paid to individuals are withheld at **5%** if the payee's current-year gross income is ₱3,000,000 or less **and** the payee's COR shows non-VAT registration. Otherwise the rate is **10%**. The ₱720,000 split for individuals is the pre-2018 rule. I could not verify the exact current rates for juridical persons (WC010/WC011) or the rental and contractor codes from sources this session.

### Cited Findings

- **Confirmed (RR 11-2018):** professional, talent and commission fees paid to individual payees are withheld at 5% if gross income for the current year does not exceed ₱3M, or 10% if it does. — [Roque Law, new withholding rules on professional, talent and commission fees](https://roquelaw.com.ph/new-withholding-rules-on-payments-of-professional-talent-and-commission-fees/); [P&A Grant Thornton tax alert (RR 11-2018)](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/alerts-and-publications/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2018/new-withholding-rules-on-payments-of-professional-talent-and-commission-fees-to--self-employed-individuals/)
- **Confirmed (RR 14-2018):** adds a second condition for the 5% rate. The payee's "BIR Certificate of Registration (COR) should indicate that he/she is non-VAT taxpayer". A VAT-registered payee is withheld at "the higher 10%" regardless of income. — [P&A Grant Thornton, additional condition for 5% EWT (RR 14-2018)](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/alerts-and-publications/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2018/additional-condition-for-5-ewt-on-professional-fees-extended-deadlines-for-sworn-declarations/)
- **Confirmed (history):** fees paid to individuals moved to 5%/10% under RR 11-2018, from 8% under RMC 1-2018 and 10%/15% before that. The old split was at ₱720,000. — [P&A Grant Thornton April 2018 Tax Brief](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/globalassets/1.-member-firms/philippines/tax-brief/april-2018-tax-brief.pdf)
- **Confirmed (code names):** RMO 38-2018 lists WI010, WI011, WC010 and WC011 for professionals such as lawyers, CPAs and engineers. The rate columns could not be read from the extract, and the PDF later returned 404. — [RMO 38-2018 (FFCCCII archive)](https://archive.ffcccii.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/RMO_No.38-2018.pdf)
- **Needs verification (conflicting sources):** a payroll guide lists WC010 at 10% and WC011 at 15% above ₱720,000. A Scribd 1601-E guideline excerpt lists WC010 at 10%. The ₱720,000 threshold for juridical persons appears in older 2307 material. — [Sprout, different tax percentages for consultants](https://guide.sprout.ph/different-tax-percentages-for-consultants); [Scribd 1601-E guidelines](https://www.scribd.com/document/440418823/BIR-Form-No-1601E-Guidelines-and-Instructions)
- **Confirmed (RR 11-2018):** an individual earning less than ₱250,000 from a single payor may be exempt from withholding if they submit the sworn declaration. — [Respicio, guide to EWT rates on professional fees](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/guide-to-expanded-withholding-tax-rates-on-professional-fees-and-commissions)

### Inferences

- **Interpretation:** WI010 corresponds to 5% (individual, ≤₱3M and non-VAT) and WI011 to 10% (individual, >₱3M or VAT-registered). This follows the RR 11-2018 / RR 14-2018 structure and the ordering in RMO 38-2018, but the code-to-rate mapping was not read directly.
- **Interpretation:** WC010 (10%, juridical gross income ≤₱720,000) and WC011 (15%, >₱720,000) appear to survive from the pre-TRAIN table for juridical payees. This is unconfirmed.

### Gaps

- **Needs verification:** rentals WI100/WC100 at 5% and contractors WI120/WC120 at 2%. No source found this session confirmed these codes or rates. They match the standard RR 2-98 Sec. 2.57.2 table from background knowledge only. Check against the current BIR ATC table or the 1601-EQ guidelines.
- No current (2025 or 2026) BIR ATC table was retrieved. The bir.gov.ph pages were not fetched.

## Q3. Sworn declaration of gross receipts (RR 11-2018)

### Takeaway

The payee individual gives each payor a sworn declaration of gross receipts with a copy of the COR, by January 15 or before the first payment. Without it the payor applies the higher 10% rate. The payor then files the declarations and a list of payees with the BIR.

### Cited Findings

- **Confirmed:** the payee submits a sworn declaration with the COR by January 15 of each year, or before the first payment for new engagements. Sources word the timing differently: "whichever is later" in one, "prior to the first payment" in another. — [Roque Law](https://roquelaw.com.ph/new-withholding-rules-on-payments-of-professional-talent-and-commission-fees/); [Respicio, current rates and compliance](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/withholding-tax-on-professionalconsultancy-fees-in-the-philippines-current-rates-and-compliance)
- **Confirmed:** if the declaration is not submitted, the higher 10% rate applies. — [Respicio](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/guide-to-expanded-withholding-tax-rates-on-professional-fees-and-commissions); [P&A Grant Thornton (RR 11-2018)](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/alerts-and-publications/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2018/new-withholding-rules-on-payments-of-professional-talent-and-commission-fees-to--self-employed-individuals/)
- **Confirmed:** the payor or withholding agent submits the declarations and a list of payees to the BIR by January 31 each year, or within 15 days after the month in which a new payee submitted one. — [Roque Law](https://roquelaw.com.ph/new-withholding-rules-on-payments-of-professional-talent-and-commission-fees/)
- **Confirmed (2018 only):** for 2018 the payee deadline was extended to April 20, 2018 and the payor deadline to April 30, 2018 (RR 14-2018). — [P&A Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/alerts-and-publications/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2018/additional-condition-for-5-ewt-on-professional-fees-extended-deadlines-for-sworn-declarations/)

### Inferences

- **Interpretation:** a payee whose gross receipts cross ₱3M mid-year should notify payors so they switch to 10%. This follows from the "current year" wording, but the mechanics were not sourced.

### Gaps

- One commentary mentions an "RR 14-2023" amendment. Its effect on these deadlines was not verified.

## Q4. BIR Form 2307 issuance timing

### Takeaway

The payor issues Form 2307 within 20 days after the close of each quarter (April 20, July 20, October 20, January 20), or together with the payment if the payee asks.

### Cited Findings

- **Confirmed (secondary source, citing RR 2-98 Sec. 2.58 as amended by RR 11-2018):** the payor issues the 2307 within 20 days after the quarter closes, so April 20, July 20, October 20 and January 20. On the payee's request it may be issued at the same time as the income payment. — [Respicio, is BIR Form 2307 issued in advance?](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/is-bir-form-2307-issued-in-advance); [Respicio, 2307 period covered](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/bir-form-2307-period-covered-explained-in-the-philippines)

### Gaps

- The text of RR 2-98 Sec. 2.58(B) itself was not retrieved. Not checked: whether RA 11976 or RR 4-2024 changed the timing.

## Q5. Return deadlines: 0619-E, 1601-EQ, 1604-E, QAP and SAWT

### Takeaway

- **0619-E:** 10th of the following month for non-eFPS filers; 15th for eFPS filers. Filed for the first two months of each quarter only.
- **1601-EQ:** last day of the month after the quarter ends.
- **1604-E:** March 1 of the following year, with the alphalist of payees.

### Cited Findings

- **Confirmed (secondary sources):** 0619-E is due by the 10th of the following month (non-eFPS). eFPS withholding agents file by the 15th of the following month under RR 11-2018. — [Respicio, 1601-E due date](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/bir-form-1601-e-due-date-in-the-philippines-what-taxpayers-need-to-know); [KPMG Special InTAX, Sept 2018](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/home/insights/2018/09/special-intax-sept2018-issue1-vol1.html) (snippet)
- **Confirmed:** 1601-EQ is due on the last day of the month following the quarter: April 30, July 31, October 31 and January 31. — [juan.tax 1601-EQ](https://juan.tax/form-1601eq/); [Respicio](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/bir-form-1601-e-due-date-in-the-philippines-what-taxpayers-need-to-know)
- **Confirmed:** 1604-E is due on or before March 1 of the following year. — [Roque Law](https://roquelaw.com.ph/updated-filing-guidelines-for-bir-forms-1604c-1604f-and-1604e-submission-of-bir-form-2316-without-employee-signature-shall-be-accepted/); [P&A Grant Thornton RMC 18-2020 PDF](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/globalassets/1.-member-firms/philippines/tax-alerts/2020/03.02.2020/rmc-no.-18-2020.pdf)
- **Confirmed (penalty context):** RR 2-2015 and related issuances impose penalties for not submitting alphalists and SLSPs. — [P&A Grant Thornton, penalties for non-submission of SLSPs and alphalists](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/alerts-and-publications/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2015/penalties-for-nonsubmission-of-slsps-alphalists-and-other-violations/) (not fetched)

### Gaps

- **Needs verification:** the QAP (Quarterly Alphalist of Payees) is an attachment to 1601-EQ, and the SAWT (Summary Alphalist of Withholding Taxes) is an attachment to the payee's 1701Q, 1701, 2551Q or 2550Q. Both are submitted through eSubmission or eFPS in DAT format. This is from background knowledge only; no source was retrieved this session.

## Q6. E-marketplace operators and DFSPs (RR 16-2023)

### Takeaway

E-marketplace operators and digital financial service providers withhold **1% on one-half of gross remittances** to sellers, an effective 0.5%. The ATCs are **WI760** (individuals) and **WC760** (corporations). There is a **₱500,000** annual gross remittance threshold. A seller under the threshold avoids withholding only by submitting a BIR-received sworn declaration by January 20 each year. The RR took effect **January 11, 2024**, not December 27, 2024. December 27, 2023 is the publication date. E-marketplace enforcement began July 15, 2024 and DFSP enforcement October 13, 2024.

### Cited Findings

- **Confirmed (RR 16-2023):** all e-marketplace operators and DFSPs withhold 1% on one-half of gross remittances to online sellers for goods and services sold and paid through the platform. "Gross remittance" excludes sales returns and discounts, VAT, and fees for use of the platform. The regulation amends RR 2-98 and RR 11-2018. It took effect "January 11, 2024, or 15 days from December 27, 2023". — [P&A Grant Thornton, online sellers subject to 1% EWT](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/tax-notes/online-sellers-subject-to-1-expanded-withholding-tax/); [Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/tax/philippines-bureau-of-internal-revenue-issues-regulations-on-withholding-tax-on-remittances-by-online-platforms-to-online-sellersmerchants)
- **Confirmed (date conflict noted):** Deloitte says RR 16-2023 was issued on December 21, 2023. One source gives January 12, 2024 as the effective date instead of January 11. — [Deloitte, Taxing the digital frontier](https://www.deloitte.com/southeast-asia/en/services/tax/perspectives/taxing-digital-frontier.html)
- **Confirmed (threshold):** no withholding applies if the seller's total gross remittances for the past year did not exceed ₱500,000, or if cumulative remittances in the current year have not exceeded ₱500,000. No withholding also applies if the seller is exempt or entitled to a lower rate and presents proof. — [P&A Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/tax-notes/online-sellers-subject-to-1-expanded-withholding-tax/)
- **Confirmed (RMC 8-2024, 15 Jan 2024):** a seller at or below ₱500,000 must give the operator a sworn declaration received by the BIR (Annex "A" template), "on or before the 20th day of the first month of each taxable year". If it is not submitted, the operator withholds automatically "regardless of the actual total income or gross remittance". Sellers must register with the BIR and give the platform a copy of their COR. — [Forvis Mazars, BIR RMC 8-2024](https://www.forvismazars.com/ph/en/insights/tax-alerts/bir-rmc-8-2024); [Maya, RMC 8-2024 Annex A template](https://www.maya.ph/hubfs/File-upload/03%202024/RMC%20No.%208-2024%20Annex%20A%20Template.pdf); [BDO](https://www.bdo.global/en-gb/insights/tax/world-wide-tax/philippines-bir-clarifies-withholding-tax-obligations-on-e-marketplaces)
- **Confirmed (ATC, RMO 4-2024, 5 Feb 2024):** the ATCs are WI760 (individuals) and WC760 (corporations) at 1%, applied to "one-half (1/2) of the gross remittances" by e-marketplace operators. A search summary calls it RMC 4-2024 while the KPMG page calls it RMO 4-2024; KPMG's version is used here. — [KPMG Special InTAX, Feb 2024](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/insights/2024/02/special-intax-february-2024-issue-1-volume-1.html)
- **Confirmed (transition):** RMC 55-2024 extended the transitory period to July 14, 2024. E-marketplace operators began withholding July 15, 2024 and DFSPs October 13, 2024. — [Forvis Mazars RMC 8-2024 alert (search snippet)](https://www.forvismazars.com/ph/en/insights/tax-alerts/bir-rmc-8-2024); [P&A Grant Thornton, DFSP deadline extension](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/tax-notes/bir-extends-the-deadline-for-the-imposition-of-withholding-tax-on-payments-made-by-digital-financial-service-providers-to-online-sellers)

### Inferences

- **Interpretation:** for the engine, the e-marketplace rate is effectively 0.5% of gross remittance, net of VAT, returns, discounts and platform fees. It applies once the prior-year or cumulative current-year remittance exceeds ₱500,000, or always if no sworn declaration was given.
- **Interpretation:** the e-marketplace sworn declaration deadline (January 20, RMC 8-2024) differs from the professional fee one (January 15, RR 11-2018). Store them separately.
- **Interpretation:** WI760/WC760 withholding is creditable, so the seller receives a 2307 and credits it against income tax like other creditable withholding.

### Gaps

- No source fetched confirmed whether the platform issues the 2307 quarterly or how the seller credits it. The PwC RMC 8-2024 alert returned 403.
- Not checked: whether 2025 or 2026 issuances changed the ₱500,000 threshold or the deadlines.

## Q7. How a payee individual credits CWT on 1701Q (timing and SAWT)

### Takeaway

I found no source this session on whether CWT is credited in the quarter of payment or the quarter shown on the 2307, or on the SAWT requirement. Everything below is background knowledge that needs verification.

### Cited Findings

- **Confirmed (indirect):** a 2307 covers a specific quarter ("period covered") and is issued after each quarter closes, which links crediting to the quarter of withholding. — [Respicio, 2307 period covered](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/bir-form-2307-period-covered-explained-in-the-philippines)
- **Confirmed (indirect):** the 1701Q is filed quarterly by self-employed individuals; the Q1 deadline is May 15. — [FilePino](https://www.filepino.com/zero-business-transactions-mandatory-bir-filing-taxes/); [KPMG RMC 32-2018 (new 1701Q)](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ph/pdf/InTAX/2018/RMC_2018-32_1701Q.pdf) (not fetched)

### Inferences

- **Interpretation:** the engine should credit CWT in the quarter that the 2307's "period covered" names, which is the quarter of payment and withholding. A 2307 received late, after that quarter's 1701Q was filed, would be claimed in a later quarterly or the annual 1701.

### Gaps

- **Needs verification (background knowledge only):** RR 2-98 Sec. 2.58.3(B) is generally understood to allow creditable withholding as a credit against the payee's income tax in the quarter in which the income was earned or received. A credit is supported by the 2307, and a SAWT must be attached to claim it. The SAWT covers 1701Q, 1701, 2551Q (for percentage-tax withholding) and 2550Q. I could not retrieve the section text or an RMC on SAWT this session.
- Not checked: whether a 2307 received after the annual return was filed can be claimed in a later year. The general rule (background knowledge) is that the credit must match the year the income is reported.
- Not checked: whether EOPT or RR 4-2024 changed when the duty to withhold arises (paid versus payable), which affects which quarter a 2307 covers.
