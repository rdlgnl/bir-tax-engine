# Income recognition basis for Philippine individual taxpayers (professionals and sole proprietors) after EOPT (RA 11976): income tax, percentage tax and VAT

Status labels used below:

- **Confirmed**: read in a primary text (statute) or in a reputable tax-firm summary of the regulation, with the issuance and section named.
- **Interpretation**: my reading of the confirmed material. A CPA should sign off.
- **Needs verification**: from memory or from weak or secondary sources only. Check against the official BIR PDF before encoding it as a rule.

Research limits: bir.gov.ph and bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph PDFs returned HTTP 403, and PwC's alert also returned 403. The regulation texts (RR 3-2024, RR 4-2024, RR 7-2024, RR 8-2018, RR No. 2) were therefore read only through tax-firm summaries (Grant Thornton, KPMG, Forvis Mazars, Deloitte, MTF Counsel). The statute texts (NIRC as RA 8424, and RA 11976) were read on lawphil.net.

## Q1. Income tax: may an individual professional or sole proprietor use the cash method? Can they choose? Does the 8% option differ from graduated rates?

### Takeaway

The NIRC does not require accrual for individuals. Taxable income follows "the method of accounting regularly employed in keeping the books". By default, gross income is included in the year it is **received**, unless the taxpayer's permitted method places it in another period. A cash-basis professional is therefore allowed in principle, but the Commissioner can impose another method if the chosen one does not clearly reflect income. EOPT changed the VAT and percentage tax base. I found no source saying it amended NIRC Sec. 43-45 or forced accrual for income tax. For the 8% option, I found no BIR text that settles whether "gross sales/receipts" means invoiced or collected amounts.

### Cited Findings

- **Confirmed (NIRC Sec. 43, General Rule):** taxable income "shall be computed upon the basis of the taxpayer's annual accounting period ... in accordance with the method of accounting regularly employed in keeping the books of such taxpayer". If no method is regularly used, or the method does not clearly reflect income, the computation follows the method that in the Commissioner's opinion clearly reflects income. — [NIRC (RA 8424), lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1997/ra_8424_1997.html)
- **Confirmed (NIRC Sec. 44, Period in which items of gross income included):** gross income items "shall be included in the gross income for the taxable year in which received by the taxpayer", unless the methods permitted under Sec. 43 account for them in a different period. On death, amounts accrued up to the date of death are included in the period that includes the death. — [NIRC (RA 8424), lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1997/ra_8424_1997.html)
- **Confirmed (NIRC Sec. 45, Period for which deductions and credits taken):** deductions are taken in the year "paid or accrued" or "paid or incurred", depending on the taxpayer's method of accounting. — [NIRC (RA 8424), lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1997/ra_8424_1997.html)
- **Confirmed (case law, old numbering):** _Consolidated Mines, Inc. v. CTA_, G.R. Nos. L-18843 & 18844, Aug. 29, 1974, quotes the same three rules under the **old** Tax Code numbering: Sec. 38 (General Rules: method regularly employed in keeping the books), Sec. 39 (items included "for the taxable year in which received"), and Sec. 40 (deductions "paid or accrued" or "paid or incurred"). The Court also observed that the Philippine Tax Code had no counterpart of the U.S. provision expressly allowing each taxpayer to adopt the method most suitable to its business, or expressly allowing a hybrid method. — [Consolidated Mines v. CTA, ChanRobles](https://chanrobles.com/cralaw/1974augustdecisions.php?id=284)
- **Confirmed (case law):** _CIR v. Isabela Cultural Corp._, G.R. No. 172231, Feb. 12, 2007: under the accrual method, income is recognised when the right to receive it becomes fixed, not when it is actually received; that is what separates it from the cash method. — [CIR v. Isabela Cultural Corp., ChanRobles](https://chanrobles.com/cralaw/2007februarydecisions.php?id=232); [BatasNatin summary](https://batasnatin.com/laws/gr-172231)
- **Secondary:** a Xero PH glossary says a change of accounting method is filed with the RDO on BIR Form 1905. — [Xero PH glossary](https://www.xero.com/ph/glossary/cash-vs-accrual-accounting/)
- **Secondary (opinion):** a Deloitte Tax Summit 2024 deck said the EOPT change "will align the accrual basis of accounting for both Income Tax and VAT". MTF Counsel wrote that cash-basis small and medium taxpayers "are now required to track their sales and services revenues using the accrual method of accounting". This was in a VAT context and does not say the income tax method changed. — [Deloitte Tax Summit 2024 deck](https://www2.deloitte.com/content/dam/Deloitte/ph/Documents/tax/ph-tax-deloitte-tax-summit-2024-speakers-presentations.pdf); [MTF Counsel, Feb. 2024](https://mtfcounsel.com/?p=6777)
- **Secondary (opinion):** Grant Thornton expects "no more discrepancy between the gross sales declared in the Income Tax Return (ITR) and VAT returns". That is a prediction, not a rule. — search-result snippet from [Grant Thornton PH](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/tax-notes/ease-of-paying-taxes-act-is-here-salient-changes-to-vat-and-percentage-tax-rules/)
- **Related (RR 4-2024 Sec. 7), Confirmed via KPMG:** the withholding agent's duty to withhold creditable tax now arises at the earliest of: income becoming payable; the payor accruing or recording it as an expense or asset; or the seller issuing the invoice. This amends Sec. 2.57.4 of RR 2-98. So a client may withhold, and issue BIR Form 2307, before the professional collects. — [KPMG Special InTax, April 2024](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/insights/2024/04/special-intax-april-2024-issue-1-volume-3.html)

### Inferences

- **Interpretation:** for income tax, an individual on **graduated rates** may keep cash-basis books if that method is applied consistently and "clearly reflects income" (NIRC Sec. 43-44). The default rule in Sec. 44 is already "received", so cash is the statutory baseline and accrual is a permitted alternative. Accrual is not a requirement.
- **Interpretation:** the **8% option** taxes "gross sales/receipts and other non-operating income" above PHP 250,000 instead of net income. Neither the 8% provision (NIRC Sec. 24(A)(2)(b), as amended by TRAIN) nor its implementing rule (RR 8-2018) was read in primary text here. Absent a special rule, the general timing rule (Sec. 43-44) would decide in which year a receipt counts. A cash-basis 8% taxpayer would count collections, and an accrual-basis one would count invoices.
- **Interpretation (app risk):** after EOPT, the percentage tax return (2551Q) and VAT return are invoice-based, while a cash-basis ITR is collection-based. The figures will legitimately differ at year end by the change in receivables. Collections of services rendered before April 27, 2024 add a further difference. The app should be able to reconcile ITR gross receipts against the 2551Q gross sales, because the BIR cross-matches returns.
- **Interpretation:** the brief's reference to "RR 2-40 / Sec. 38-39" probably mixes up the old Tax Code numbering (Sec. 38-40, now Sec. 43-45) with RR No. 2 (1940). I could not locate the RR No. 2 section numbers that cover accounting methods, so do not cite any of them.
- **Interpretation:** because of RR 4-2024 Sec. 7, creditable withholding tax (CWT) on a 2307 can belong to a period before the income is collected. A cash-basis app must let CWT be claimed in the year the related income is reported. Matching CWT to the related income is the usual practice; that is **Needs verification** against RR 2-98 Sec. 2.58.3.

### Gaps

- I did not obtain the text of RR No. 2 (1940) on accounting methods. Its section numbers are **Needs verification**, and none should be cited until the text is seen.
- I did not obtain RR 8-2018's definitions of "gross sales" and "gross receipts" for the 8% option, or any RMC Q&A on whether the 8% base is invoiced or collected. **Needs verification**: RR 8-2018 Sec. 2 or 3, and RMC 50-2018.
- I found no BIR ruling that specifically addresses cash-basis individuals after EOPT.
- Whether EOPT amended the wording of NIRC Sec. 24 (8% "gross sales or gross receipts") was not checked. My understanding is that it did not, which is **Needs verification**.

## Q2. Percentage tax (NIRC Sec. 116): after EOPT, is the base "gross sales" or "gross receipts" for sellers of services? Which rule says so, and from when?

### Takeaway

"Gross sales". RA 11976 Sec. 24 amended NIRC Sec. 116 to impose 3% of "gross quarterly sales", where it previously read "gross quarterly sales or receipts". RR 3-2024, not RR 4-2024, implements the change. Its general amendments replace "gross receipts" with "Gross Sales", and replace receipt- or payment-based recognition with "billing/billed", across both Title IV (VAT) and Title V (Percentage Tax). The change took effect April 27, 2024. RR 4-2024 deals with other matters.

### Cited Findings

- **Confirmed (RA 11976 Sec. 24, amending NIRC Sec. 116):** "a tax equivalent to three percent (3%) of his gross quarterly sales". The amended section does not define "gross sales" itself. — [RA 11976, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2024/ra_11976_2024.html)
- **Confirmed (KPMG):** EOPT moved the base of Sec. 116 (persons exempt from VAT), and of Sec. 117-119, from gross sales or receipts to gross sales. — [KPMG, Ease of Paying Taxes Act (Jan. 2024)](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/home/insights/2024/01/ease-of-paying-taxes-act.html) (search-result summary)
- **Confirmed via Grant Thornton (RR 3-2024, General Amendments A and C):** "gross selling price", "gross value in money" and "gross receipts" are uniformly renamed "GROSS SALES", whether goods or services. References to receipts or payments as the basis for recognising sales or services under Title IV (VAT) **and Title V (Percentage Tax)** now read "BILLING" or "BILLED". GT: "The EOPT Act adopts the accrual basis of recognizing sales for both sales of goods and services." — [Grant Thornton tax alert on RR 3-2024](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/alerts-and-publications/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2024/implementation-of-the-amendments-introduced-by-eopt-act-on-vat-and-percentage-tax/)
- **Confirmed (KPMG):** RR 3-2024 is dated April 11, 2024, was posted on the BIR website April 12, 2024, and takes effect "15 days following its publication in the Official Gazette or the BIR official website, whichever comes first". — [KPMG Special InTax, April 2024](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/insights/2024/04/special-intax-april-2024-issue-1-volume-3.html)
- **Confirmed (effective date):** the EOPT implementing RRs "collectively took effect on April 27, 2024" (Deloitte). Forvis Mazars and FilePino use the same April 26 / April 27, 2024 cutoff. — [Deloitte, Navigating the new tax landscape (May 14, 2024)](https://www.deloitte.com/southeast-asia/en/services/tax/perspectives/navigating-new-tax-landscape.html); [Forvis Mazars, transitory provisions](https://forvismazars.com/ph/en/insights/tax-alerts/ease-of-paying-taxes-act/transitory-provisions-of-eopt-act-issuances); [FilePino on RR 3-2024](https://filepino.com/tax-updates-eopt-bir-revenue-regulations-rr-3-2024/)
- **Conflict on the posting date:** Forvis Mazars says RR 3-2024 was posted on the BIR website on April 26, 2024. KPMG and GT say April 12, 2024. Both still give an April 27, 2024 effective date. — [Forvis Mazars, effectivity dates](https://forvismazars.com/ph/en/insights/tax-alerts/ease-of-paying-taxes-act/effectivity-dates-of-eopt-act-regulations); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/insights/2024/04/special-intax-april-2024-issue-1-volume-3.html)
- **Confirmed (RR 4-2024 subject):** filing and payment at any venue (Sec. 3), removal of the wrong-venue penalty (Sec. 4), OCWs/OFWs not filing an ITR (Sec. 5), removal of the withholding requirement for deductibility (Sec. 6), new timing of withholding (Sec. 7), and refund of excess CWT (Sec. 8). It does **not** set the percentage tax base. — [KPMG Special InTax, April 2024](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/insights/2024/04/special-intax-april-2024-issue-1-volume-3.html)
- **Secondary (FilePino):** percentage tax and output tax "must be remitted in the applicable filing period based on the invoice date". — [FilePino on RR 3-2024](https://filepino.com/tax-updates-eopt-bir-revenue-regulations-rr-3-2024/)
- **Confirmed (RA 11976 Sec. 48, transitory clause):** "Taxpayers are hereby given six (6) months from the effectivity of the implementing revenue regulations" to comply with the VAT and percentage tax amendments. Grant Thornton read this as running to October 27, 2024. — [RA 11976, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2024/ra_11976_2024.html); [Grant Thornton, invoicing under EOPT](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/lets-talk-tax/invoicing-requirements-under-the-eopt-act/)

### Inferences

- **Interpretation:** for non-VAT professionals and sole proprietors paying 3% under Sec. 116, sales of services invoiced on or after April 27, 2024 are counted in the quarter of the **invoice date**, whether or not they have been collected.
- **Interpretation:** the RR 3-2024 Sec. 7(a) transitory rule (Q3 below) is written for "output VAT". No source spells out an equivalent percentage tax rule for services rendered or billed before April 27, 2024 and collected later. Two things suggest the same result for the 3%: the Title V "billing" amendment applies only from effectivity, and the old Sec. 116 base was "receipts". On that reading, percentage tax on those old receivables is due **on collection**. This is **Needs verification**, though it is consistent with the logic of the prior law.
- **Rate history, Needs verification beyond these sources:** CREATE (RA 11534) cut the Sec. 116 rate to 1% for July 1, 2020 to June 30, 2023. RA 11976's amended Sec. 116 text reads 3%. — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/home/insights/2024/01/ease-of-paying-taxes-act.html) (search-result summary)

### Gaps

- I did not see the exact RR 3-2024 section that amends the percentage tax regulations, whether a Sec. 4.116 or an amendment to RR 16-2005 Title V. GT describes it only as a "General Amendment". **Needs verification** in the official PDF.
- No source gives an explicit percentage tax (non-VAT) transitory rule for pre-April 27, 2024 receivables.
- The Sec. 116 threshold and its interaction with the VAT threshold were not covered here; they belong to a separate question.

## Q3. VAT on sale of services (NIRC Sec. 108): base change, effective date, RR 3-2024, transitory rule for pre-effectivity receivables

### Takeaway

RA 11976 Sec. 17 amended Sec. 108 so that VAT on services is levied on "gross sales", which is invoice- or billing-based, instead of "gross receipts". RR 3-2024 implements it from **April 27, 2024**. Transitory rule, RR 3-2024 Sec. 7(a): for services rendered and billed but uncollected before the RR took effect, output VAT is declared in the quarterly VAT return **when collected**, supported by an invoice. Sec. 7(b): no output VAT credit is allowed on pre-effectivity uncollected receivables from goods. Going forward, the seller may deduct output VAT on uncollected receivables once the agreed payment period lapses (new Sec. 110(D), RR 3-2024 Sec. 5 adding Sec. 4.110-9 to RR 16-2005).

### Cited Findings

- **Confirmed (RA 11976 Sec. 17, amending NIRC Sec. 108(A)):** gross sales for services means "the total amount of money or its equivalent representing the contract price, compensation, service fee, rental or royalty". For long-term contracts, "the invoice shall be issued on the month in which the service, or use or lease of properties is rendered or supplied". — [RA 11976, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2024/ra_11976_2024.html)
- **Confirmed via KPMG (RR 3-2024 Sec. 2):** the amended term "Gross Sales" applies uniformly throughout RR 16-2005. — [KPMG Special InTax, April 2024](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/insights/2024/04/special-intax-april-2024-issue-1-volume-3.html)
- **Confirmed via KPMG (RR 3-2024 Sec. 7(a)):** for billed but uncollected sales of services rendered before the RR took effect, "the corresponding output VAT shall be declared once it has been collected". The sale must be supported by an invoice under the RR 7-2024 transitory provisions, or by a new BIR-approved invoice, as applicable. — [KPMG Special InTax, April 2024](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/insights/2024/04/special-intax-april-2024-issue-1-volume-3.html); same in [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/alerts-and-publications/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2024/implementation-of-the-amendments-introduced-by-eopt-act-on-vat-and-percentage-tax/) ("shall only be declared in the quarterly VAT return when the collection was made") and [Forvis Mazars](https://forvismazars.com/ph/en/insights/tax-alerts/ease-of-paying-taxes-act/transitory-provisions-of-eopt-act-issuances)
- **Secondary (FilePino timing table):** services billed and collected on or before April 26, 2024 follow the old official-receipt rules. Services billed on or before April 26, 2024 but collected on or after April 27, 2024 are taxed on payment and supported by an invoice issued on payment. — [FilePino on RR 3-2024](https://filepino.com/tax-updates-eopt-bir-revenue-regulations-rr-3-2024/)
- **Confirmed via KPMG and GT (RR 3-2024 Sec. 7(b), Sec. 4.110-9):** "No output tax credit shall be allowed for outstanding receivables from sale of goods on account prior to the effectivity" of the RR. The credit applies only to transactions on or after effectivity. — [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/alerts-and-publications/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2024/implementation-of-the-amendments-introduced-by-eopt-act-on-vat-and-percentage-tax/); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/insights/2024/04/special-intax-april-2024-issue-1-volume-3.html)
- **Confirmed (RA 11976 Sec. 19, NIRC Sec. 110(D)):** "A seller of goods or services may deduct the output VAT pertaining to uncollected receivables". MTF Counsel's summary gives the conditions: the deduction is taken from output VAT of the next quarter after the agreed payment period lapses; the seller has fully paid the VAT on the transaction; and the VAT component has not been claimed as a bad-debt deduction under Sec. 34(E). On recovery, the VAT is added back to output VAT in the period of recovery. — [RA 11976, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2024/ra_11976_2024.html); [MTF Counsel](https://mtfcounsel.com/?p=6777)
- **Confirmed via KPMG (RR 3-2024 Sec. 5):** adds Sec. 4.110-9 to RR 16-2005 on the uncollected-receivables credit, and states that these rules do not affect bad-debt deductibility for income tax under RR 25-2002. — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/insights/2024/04/special-intax-april-2024-issue-1-volume-3.html)
- **Confirmed (Deloitte):** "There will be a shift from cash to accrual basis for purposes of VAT recognition under RR 3-2024", making the invoice "the sole document required in declaring output taxes and claiming input taxes". — [Deloitte, May 14, 2024](https://www.deloitte.com/southeast-asia/en/services/tax/perspectives/navigating-new-tax-landscape.html)
- **Confirmed (RA 11976 Sec. 51):** the Act takes effect 15 days after publication in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation. — [RA 11976, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2024/ra_11976_2024.html)

### Inferences

- **Interpretation (app rule):** a VAT-registered individual's services are counted for output VAT in the period of the **invoice date** if invoiced on or after April 27, 2024, and in the period of the **collection date** if rendered or billed on or before April 26, 2024. Each sale record needs the rendered or service date, the invoice date and the collection dates to apply this.
- **Interpretation:** the uncollected-receivables credit is a separate, later adjustment. It applies only to post-effectivity sales, and only after the agreed credit term lapses. It reverses on recovery.

### Gaps

- The statute's own effective date was not read on the page. It is widely cited as January 22, 2024 (published January 5, 2024), which is **Needs verification**.
- The exact text of RR 3-2024 Sec. 7 was not seen in primary form because the BIR PDF returned 403. The wording above comes from three tax-firm summaries that agree.
- A BIR RMC on availing the uncollected-receivables credit exists (Grant Thornton article title: "EOPT is here: BIR clarifies availment of output VAT credit on uncollected receivables"). I did not read it, and its number is **Needs verification**. — [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/tax-notes/eopt-is-here-bir-clarifies-availment-of-output-vat-credit-on-uncollected-receivables/)

## Q4. Books of accounts: should a cash-basis individual's books record income when received or when invoiced? Does EOPT's invoice rule force accrual books?

### Takeaway

No source says EOPT forces accrual books for income tax. NIRC Sec. 43 makes the books' regular method govern income tax, and EOPT's "billing" or "gross sales" amendments are confined to Title IV (VAT) and Title V (Percentage Tax). A cash-basis individual can keep recording income when it is received for income tax purposes. They must still capture invoice-date sales, since the VAT and percentage tax returns now run on invoices, so in practice the records need both views.

### Cited Findings

- **Confirmed (NIRC Sec. 43):** income is computed "in accordance with the method of accounting regularly employed in keeping the books of such taxpayer". — [NIRC, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1997/ra_8424_1997.html)
- **Confirmed via GT (RR 3-2024 General Amendment C):** the "billing/billed" substitution is expressly limited to recognising sales "under Title IV (VAT) and Title V (Percentage Tax)". — [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/alerts-and-publications/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2024/implementation-of-the-amendments-introduced-by-eopt-act-on-vat-and-percentage-tax/)
- **Secondary (MTF Counsel):** cash-basis taxpayers "are now required to track their sales and services revenues using the accrual method of accounting", and service providers must "advance the VAT even before they are paid". This is in a VAT context. — [MTF Counsel](https://mtfcounsel.com/?p=6777)
- **Confirmed via KPMG (RR 3-2024 Sec. 5):** the output VAT credit rules do not affect income tax bad-debt deductibility under RR 25-2002. This keeps VAT and income tax treatment of the same receivable separate. — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/insights/2024/04/special-intax-april-2024-issue-1-volume-3.html)
- **Confirmed via GT (RR 7-2024, CAS/CBA users):** computerised accounting systems must be reconfigured so that output VAT on services is reported "upon rendition of services and not upon collection". This is treated as a major system enhancement requiring a new Acknowledgement Certificate. — [Grant Thornton, invoicing under EOPT](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/lets-talk-tax/invoicing-requirements-under-the-eopt-act/); deadlines as amended by RR 11-2024 in [Forvis Mazars](https://forvismazars.com/ph/en/insights/tax-alerts/ease-of-paying-taxes-act/transitory-provisions-of-eopt-act-issuances) (adjustments by December 31, 2024; extension of up to six months)

### Inferences

- **Interpretation:** a cash-basis individual's journal and ledger may record service income when collected, provided that is the method regularly employed. The sales book or invoice register must still list every invoice by date, because the VAT and percentage tax returns are now built from invoices. A cash-method professional who is VAT-registered or pays percentage tax therefore needs an invoice-date view and a cash-received view of the same sales.
- **Interpretation (app design):** store each sale with its invoice date and its collection events. Drive the 2550Q and 2551Q from invoice date (with the April 27, 2024 transitory exception). Drive the ITR (1701Q / 1701 / 1701A) from the taxpayer's chosen method per tax year: collection under cash, invoice under accrual. Record the method choice per year, since a change may need BIR notice (Form 1905). The Form 1905 requirement is secondary-sourced and **Needs verification**.
- **Interpretation:** an accrual-basis professional would see invoice-date amounts agree across the ITR and the 2551Q or 2550Q. A cash-basis one will show a timing difference equal to the change in receivables.

### Gaps

- I did not read NIRC Sec. 232 (books required, simplified books for small taxpayers, audited FS thresholds) or Sec. 235 (retention, which EOPT reportedly set at five years). **Needs verification** of the current text.
- I found no BIR ruling or RMC that states whether non-CAS manual-books taxpayers must change their income tax method.

## Q5. Invoice timing for sale of services under EOPT (RR 7-2024)

### Takeaway

From April 27, 2024 the **invoice** replaces the official receipt as the primary evidence of a sale of services. Under amended NIRC Sec. 237(A) it must be issued "at the point of each sale and transfer of merchandise or for services rendered". For long-term contracts it is issued in the month the service is rendered (Sec. 108(A) proviso). An OR or acknowledgement receipt may still be issued on collection, but only as a supplementary document that cannot support an input VAT claim.

### Cited Findings

- **Confirmed (RA 11976 Sec. 35, amending NIRC Sec. 237(A)):** invoices shall be issued "at the point of each sale and transfer of merchandise or for services rendered". — [RA 11976, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2024/ra_11976_2024.html)
- **Confirmed (RA 11976 Sec. 21, amending NIRC Sec. 113(A)):** "A VAT-registered person shall issue a VAT invoice for every sale, barter, exchange, or lease of goods or properties", and per RMC 77-2024 "and for every sale, barter or exchange of services". — [RA 11976, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2024/ra_11976_2024.html); [BatasNatin on RMC 77-2024](https://batasnatin.com/laws/rmc-no-77-2024-clarification-on-the-invoicing-requirements-provided-under-rev)
- **Confirmed (RA 11976 Sec. 17, NIRC Sec. 108(A) proviso):** for long-term contracts, "the invoice shall be issued on the month in which the service, or use or lease of properties is rendered or supplied". — [RA 11976, lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2024/ra_11976_2024.html)
- **Confirmed (RR 7-2024 dates):** issued March 22, 2024 and effective April 27, 2024. From then, issuing an OR as the primary document for a service sale is treated as failure to issue an invoice. A seller who already invoiced may issue an OR or acknowledgement receipt on collection instead of a second invoice. — [Grant Thornton, invoicing under EOPT](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/lets-talk-tax/invoicing-requirements-under-the-eopt-act/); [Triple i Consulting, invoice vs OR under RR 7-2024](https://www.tripleiconsulting.com/?p=6904) (search-result summaries)
- **Confirmed via Forvis Mazars (RR 7-2024 as amended by RR 11-2024):**
    - Unused ORs could be used as supplementary documents stamped "THIS DOCUMENT IS NOT VALID FOR CLAIM OF INPUT TAX".
    - ORs could instead be converted into invoices by striking out "Official Receipt" and stamping "Invoice"; these are valid from April 27, 2024 until consumed.
    - The inventory of unused ORs was due July 31, 2024.
    - Machine- or system-generated ORs remain valid until December 31, 2024 or until reconfiguration is complete, whichever is earlier.
    - Issuing unconverted ORs for sales after December 31, 2024 (or unconverted manual ORs from April 27, 2024) is "tantamount to failure to issue or non-issuance of Invoice", penalised under NIRC Sec. 264(a).
    - Source: [Forvis Mazars, transitory provisions](https://forvismazars.com/ph/en/insights/tax-alerts/ease-of-paying-taxes-act/transitory-provisions-of-eopt-act-issuances); also [PwC Tax Alert No. 27 on RR 11-2024](https://pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/tax-alerts/2024/tax-alert-27.html) (not opened)
- **Confirmed via GT:** for collections of services rendered before effectivity, an invoice must still be issued so the buyer can claim input VAT. — [Grant Thornton, invoicing under EOPT](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/lets-talk-tax/invoicing-requirements-under-the-eopt-act/)
- **Secondary:** the mandatory invoice fields are listed in RR 7-2024 Sec. 6(B). "Business style" was removed. — search-result summary of [Triple i Consulting](https://www.tripleiconsulting.com/?p=6904)

### Inferences

- **Interpretation:** for a non-long-term service engagement, the invoice is due when the service is rendered, at the point of sale, not when payment arrives. The invoice date is therefore close to the date the service is rendered. An app that lets users invoice at collection would understate VAT and percentage tax in earlier periods.
- **Interpretation:** for retainers and milestone billing, invoice monthly or per milestone in the month the service is rendered (Sec. 108(A) proviso), which ties tax timing to the service month.
